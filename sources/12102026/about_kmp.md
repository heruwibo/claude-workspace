Koperasi Merah Putih: what it is and how it’s going
Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih (KDKMP) is a nationwide Indonesian government initiative to establish or strengthen cooperatives in villages and urban wards. The idea is to make each cooperative a locally rooted business and service hub—selling essential goods, supporting producers, and connecting residents to finance, logistics, and some public services. The central tension is that it is meant to be community-owned, but it has been launched through a large, centrally directed program.
Purpose and intended activities
The program was established under Presidential Instruction No. 9 of 2025, which directed the government to accelerate the creation, development, or revitalization of 80,000 village and urban-ward cooperatives. The instruction calls for activities such as essential-goods retail, savings and loans, clinics, pharmacies, cold storage, warehousing, and logistics, while taking local conditions and existing village businesses into account.[peraturan.bpk.go][setneg.go]
The intended economic role is broader than running a shop:
Distribution: sell staples and, where authorized, help distribute subsidized goods such as fertilizer or LPG.
Market access: collect, store, process, or market local products from farmers, fishers, livestock producers, craftspeople, and small businesses.
Services: potentially provide member financing, logistics, health-related services, and other facilities suited to local needs.
Local economic coordination: work alongside existing cooperatives, village-owned enterprises (BUMDes), traders, and producer groups—not simply replace them.[setneg.go][antaranews]
The name “Merah Putih” means “Red and White,” the colors of Indonesia’s flag.
How it was formed and financed
The policy allows three routes: create a new cooperative where none exists, develop an existing one, or revive an inactive cooperative. The national launch of 80,081 cooperative institutions took place in July 2025. That figure refers to launched institutions, not necessarily cooperatives with functioning premises, active members, or sustained business.[peraturan.bpk.go][kemenkeu.go]
The financing arrangements have changed. The original 2025 framework included Himbara-bank loans for cooperatives. PMK 15/2026, effective April 1, 2026, replaced the earlier financing regulations and set out a different mechanism for funding construction of cooperative outlets, warehouses, and related facilities. Its title and scope cover the use of village funds or local-government transfers for that construction.[jdih.kemenkeu.go]
In the current operational framework, Presidential Regulation No. 82 of 2026 addresses governance and operations. The Ministry of Cooperatives says it provides for a two-year transitional operating assignment to state-owned Agrinas Pangan Nusantara, including day-to-day operations, staffing, business development, mentoring, and integration with the Simkopdes information system. It also identifies possible capital sources such as member contributions, reserves, grants, loans, government support, and partnerships.[antaranews]
The physical assets and debt arrangements are an important point to check locally. In October 2026, the Minister of Cooperatives said buildings in villages are village property and should be formally handed over. That makes written asset records and a clear division of responsibility especially important.[antaranews]
What the numbers do—and don’t—show
The rollout has moved faster in legal formation than in business activation. An October 2026 ANTARA analysis, citing Simkopdes data accessed September 5, reported 83,363 cooperatives with legal status, about 27,589 physical outlets built, and more than Rp207 billion in recorded transactions among operating outlets. Those figures describe different stages, so the legal count should not be read as the number of active businesses.[jatim.antaranews]
The same analysis found that reported sales were dominated by widely distributed goods such as medium rice, cooking oil, and fertilizer; it said local-product contributions appeared small. That raises a key question: will these cooperatives build local supply chains and producer markets, or mainly function as a national chain of government-supported essential-goods shops?[jatim.antaranews]
In East Java—particularly relevant to you in Surabaya—8,494 cooperatives had been registered across the province’s villages and urban wards. An earlier provincial report recorded Rp62.12 billion in transactions from those cooperatives by July 21, 2026, but that is a province-wide transaction figure, not a measure of profit or financial viability.[jatim.antaranews][surabaya.bisnis]
Main promises and risks
Potential benefits: If genuinely member-led and adapted to local demand, a KDKMP could aggregate small producers’ output, improve access to inputs and markets, reduce some distribution bottlenecks, and retain more value in the local economy. The government also presents the cooperatives as a way to connect village production to larger supply chains.[setneg.go][antaranews]
Main risks and criticisms:
Top-down formation: Critics argue that centrally accelerated formation can conflict with the cooperative principle of being created and controlled by members. ANTARA’s October analysis says the next test is whether the operating model becomes more bottom-up and locally tailored.[jatim.antaranews]
Weak commercial viability: A building or legal entity does not prove there is enough sales volume, margin, working capital, competent management, or member participation to cover operating costs.
One-size-fits-all businesses: A general-goods outlet may suit one locality, while another may be better served by crop handling, fisheries cold-chain services, feed and farm inputs, processing, or marketing.
Competition with existing businesses: If government-backed outlets sell the same goods as local shops or overlap with BUMDes and established cooperatives, they could displace rather than strengthen local economic activity.[jatim.antaranews]
Governance and procurement: A large program involving construction, vehicles, equipment, staffing, and multiple agencies needs transparent procurement, clear asset ownership, effective audits, and public reporting. BBC reported allegations and concerns about procurement transparency, questionable sites, staffing, and potential corruption risks. These are reported concerns and allegations—not proof that every cooperative or transaction is improper.[bbc]
Local participation and accountability: The cooperative needs active members, regular member meetings, reliable accounting, and meaningful oversight. A cooperative that mainly carries out instructions from outside risks becoming an administrative distribution arm rather than a member-owned enterprise.
How to assess one in practice
For an individual KDKMP, the practical questions are more informative than whether it has a signboard or legal certificate:
Who are the members, and do they participate in decisions? Check membership records, member meetings, voting, and whether members understand the cooperative’s business.
What is the actual business model? Identify customers, suppliers, product margins, expected turnover, working-capital needs, and how the business fits local production and demand.
What is operating now? Distinguish planned services from services actually open, stocked, staffed, and used.
Who owns the assets and bears the liabilities? Review the formal handover documents, land status, inventory ownership, loan or funding agreements, and who is responsible for repayment and maintenance.
Are the accounts and procurement auditable? Ask for sales, gross margins, operating expenses, cash position, contracts, and the latest member-approved annual report.
Does it complement existing local institutions? Map its relationship with BUMDes, local shops, producer groups, existing cooperatives, and distributors.
Bottom line: Koperasi Merah Putih is a very large state-backed effort to build a cooperative presence across Indonesia’s villages and urban wards. Its success will depend less on how many entities are registered or buildings constructed, and more on whether each cooperative becomes a transparent, member-controlled, locally relevant business that can operate sustainably without undermining the local enterprises it is supposed to support.[antaranews][jatim.antaranews]
