Here is a curated “legendary” reading list for project management—books that are widely treated as foundational, standards, or enduring classics. PMI’s historical survey also identifies several early classics that shaped the discipline, especially around CPM/PERT and systems management. [pmi](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/basic-project-management-reference-library-5664)

## Foundational classics

| Book | Author(s) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Management: A Systems Approach to Planning, Scheduling, and Controlling** | Harold Kerzner | Probably the most widely used comprehensive PM textbook; strong on planning, scheduling, control, and organizational context.  [libguides.library.qut.edu](https://libguides.library.qut.edu.au/projectmanage/books) |
| **Project Management: The Managerial Process** | Erik Larson & Clifford Gray | A standard university text balancing technical tools with managerial practice. |
| **Project Management for Engineering, Business and Technology** | John M. Nicholas & Herman Steyn | Particularly useful for engineering and industrial projects. |
| **Project Management: A Practical Guide** | Roel Grit | Accessible, practice-oriented introduction.  [libguides.library.qut.edu](https://libguides.library.qut.edu.au/projectmanage/books) |
| **Project Management for the Unofficial Project Manager** | Kory Kogon, Suzette Blakemore & James Wood | Excellent for engineers/managers who lead projects without formal PM titles. |
| **The Fast Forward MBA in Project Management** | Eric Verzuh | A compact, highly practical reference for tools, plans, and stakeholder management. |

## Standards and bodies of knowledge

| Literature | Publisher | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| **A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)** | PMI | The canonical PMI standard for processes, knowledge areas, and terminology. |
| **The Standard for Program Management** | PMI | Essential when coordinating multiple related projects as a program.  [ucsd.libguides](https://ucsd.libguides.com/c.php?g=90939&p=583957) |
| **The Standard for Portfolio Management** | PMI | For selecting, prioritizing, and governing a portfolio of projects. |
| **PRINCE2** guidance | AXELOS | Process-based project governance, common in UK/Commonwealth and government environments. |
| **ISO 21500 / ISO 21502** | ISO | International guidance and standard for project, programme, and portfolio management. |
| **Agile Practice Guide** | PMI & Agile Alliance | Bridges traditional PM with Agile delivery methods. |

## Agile, Lean, and modern delivery

| Book | Author(s) | Why it is influential |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrum: The Art of Doing Twice the Work in Half the Time** | Jeff Sutherland | Core explanation of Scrum from one of its co-creators. |
| **The Scrum Guide** | Ken Schwaber & Jeff Sutherland | The short, authoritative definition of Scrum. |
| **Agile Project Management with Scrum** | Ken Schwaber | Practical Scrum implementation for project managers. |
| **The Lean Startup** | Eric Ries | Foundational for iterative product development, MVPs, and validated learning. |
| **The Phoenix Project** | Gene Kim, Kevin Behr & George Spafford | A novel that teaches DevOps, constraints, flow, and IT-project failure modes. |
| **The Goal** | Eliyahu Goldratt | Classic on Theory of Constraints; highly relevant to manufacturing and delivery throughput. |
| **Critical Chain** | Eliyahu Goldratt | Applies constraint thinking to project scheduling and buffers. |
| **Making Work Visible** | Dominica DeGrandis | Practical Kanban and workflow-management guidance. |

| Book | Author(s) | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Management with CPM and PERT** | Joseph J. Moder & Cecil R. Phillips | Historic foundational work on network scheduling; one of the most recognized early PM references.  [pmi](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/basic-project-management-reference-library-5664) |

| **Project Management, Planning and Control** | Albert Lester | Strong engineering/construction-oriented planning reference. |

| **Effective Project Management: Traditional, Agile, Extreme, Hybrid** | Robert K. Wysocki | Useful when choosing the right delivery model for different project types.  [guides.erau](https://guides.erau.edu/project-management/books) |
| **Project Management ToolBox** | Cynthia Snyder Dionisio & Russ J. Martinelli | Practical collection of tools and techniques for practicing PMs.  [libguides.library.qut.edu](https://libguides.library.qut.edu.au/projectmanage/books) |

|---|---|---|

## Recommended reading path

For an engineering/industrial project context, I would read them in this order:

1. **Kerzner** — build the full technical and managerial foundation. [libguides.library.qut.edu](https://libguides.library.qut.edu.au/projectmanage/books)
2. **PMBOK Guide + Agile Practice Guide** — learn the standard vocabulary and hybrid delivery methods.  
3. **The Goal** and **Critical Chain** — understand constraints, throughput, buffers, and realistic scheduling.  
4. **The Phoenix Project** — connect projects to operations, DevOps, and organizational bottlenecks.  
5. **O’Brien’s CPM in Construction Management** or **Lester’s Project Management, Planning and Control** — for shipbuilding, EPC, infrastructure, and industrial capital projects. [pmi](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/basic-project-management-reference-library-5664)
6. **The Art of Project Management / Making Things Happen** — develop stakeholder, team, and execution leadership.
