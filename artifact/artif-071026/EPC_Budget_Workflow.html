01

Quotation: estimating and bidding

Turn the client's scope into a price you can defend, with the risks costed.

  1. Read the scope. Go through the invitation to bid, specs, drawings, contract terms, payment terms and liquidated damages, and log clarifications and exclusions.
  2. Build the estimate bottom-up:
    • Engineering: man-hours per deliverable × rates.
    • Procurement: equipment from vendor quotes, bulk materials from material take-offs (MTO), freight, duties.
    • Construction: labour hours × productivity × rates, plus equipment and subcontracts.
    • Indirects: site overhead, temporary facilities, insurance, bonds, financing.
    • Escalation and foreign-exchange exposure.
  3. Cost the risk. Use a risk register to set contingency for known unknowns, then add margin.
  4. Map to the client's format. The internal estimate is coded by WBS and cost element, but the price goes to the client in their bid schedule or pay-item format. Keep a cross-reference between the two.
  5. Get approval. Run bid/no-bid and tender review against the delegation-of-authority matrix, then submit.
Output

The priced quotation plus a frozen estimate baseline.

02

Contract award and the internal budget

Convert the contract value into a budget the project can be held to.

  1. Tender-to-contract reconciliation: compare the negotiated price with the estimate. Find where margin or contingency was given away and which scope changed.
  2. Original Control Budget (OCB): split the contract value into cost budget, contingency and margin. Management reserve sits outside the project.
  3. Time-phase the budget against the schedule to get the cost baseline (S-curve) and the cash-flow forecast: when you pay versus when you get paid.
  4. Load it into the ERP (SAP PS, Oracle, etc.) as WBS elements or cost codes with budgets, and switch on availability control so you can't commit more than the budget.

  • Control account = a WBS element × an OBS owner. It is the level where budget, schedule and progress are managed and reported.
  • Work package = the lowest level of the WBS. Each has a budget, dates and a progress-measurement method: weighted milestones, units installed, or % complete for level-of-effort work.

How the structures map in practice

SituationHow the interlock handles it
One PO, many PBS itemsA pipe or valve package buys for dozens of tags. Each PO line (or split account assignment) keeps its own WBS + PBS code, so the commitment is spread to the right accounts.
One work package, many POsA piping work package draws material from several procurement packages. The cost report rolls all of them up by WBS; the material status rolls up by PBS.
Procurement packagesBuyers group MTO lines by commodity (pipe, valves, steel, cable) into packages for RFQ. The package is the buying unit; the WBS × PBS code on each line is still the costing unit.
Bulk and stock materialBought to a warehouse against an area or discipline WBS, then issued to a work package and PBS item. Cost moves to the work package at issue, not at purchase.
Engineering revisionA new MTO revision changes the required quantity per PBS item. The difference becomes a delta PR (shortage) or a surplus. Quantity growth above the estimate is logged as a trend in section 07.
Free-issue to subcontractorsMaterial bought by the EPC contractor and issued to a subcontractor stays coded to the PBS item, so installed quantity can be reconciled against what was issued.
Rule

No PR line without a WBS element and a PBS item. If engineering hasn't produced an MTO line for it, the request has no quantity basis and no budget home. That points to a scope or estimate gap to resolve first.

  • Track commitments, not just actuals. Roughly 60–70% of EPC cost is locked in by POs and subcontracts, so commitments give you the early warning.
  • Receipt: materials go through expediting, inspection and test plans and factory acceptance tests, then a goods receipt (GR) at site. Services and subcontracts use a service entry sheet or progress certificate.
  • Vendor advance payments are made only against an advance-payment bank guarantee and recovered from later invoices.
05

Accounts payable control

Pay only for what was ordered and received, at the agreed price.

POprice and quantity ordered
GR / certificatequantity received or work done
Invoiceamount billed
  1. The vendor submits an invoice.
  2. Three-way match: PO = GR or service entry sheet = invoice. Any mismatch is held.
  3. The invoice is approved per the delegation of authority and posted as an actual cost to the cost code.
  4. Deductions: retention on subcontractors (typically 5–10%), advance-payment recovery, withholding tax (PPh 23 / PPh 4(2) for construction services), and VAT (PPN).
  5. Payment is made per the payment terms and scheduled to match cash coming in from the client.
  6. Accruals: goods received but not yet invoiced are accrued, so actual costs aren't understated at month-end.

Key controls

No PO without a PR
No payment without a GR or certificate
No PO raised after the work has started
No single person requests, approves and pays
06

Accounts receivable control

Bill everything you are entitled to, as early as the contract allows, and collect it.

Billing elementHow it works
Advance paymentUsually 10–20%, issued against an advance-payment bond and recovered pro-rata from each claim
Milestone billingPaid on defined events, e.g. design approval, equipment delivery, mechanical completion
Progress billingA monthly progress claim based on measured progress, certified by the client or engineer
Retention5–10% withheld from each claim; half is often released at handover and the rest after the defects liability period
Variation ordersBilled only after formal approval, so chase approvals quickly
ClaimsDisputed extra costs or extensions of time, tracked separately
  • Ageing and days sales outstanding (DSO).
  • Unbilled work (done but not claimed) is a contract asset under PSAK 72 / IFRS 15. Billing in excess of progress is a contract liability.
  • Revenue recognition is usually over time, using cost-to-cost % complete.
  • Stay cash-positive: collections should run ahead of AP payments. Front-loaded milestones help.

08

Close-out

Close every commitment, collect every receivable, and feed the real numbers back to estimating.

  • Contracts: close out all POs and subcontracts and agree final accounts with vendors.
  • Money: collect final retention, release bonds, and settle open claims and VOs.
  • Reconciliation: check final cost against the estimate per PBS item and WBS element.
  • Feedback: put actual unit rates, productivity and lessons learned into the estimating database, so the next quotation is better.

Where EPC projects usually lose control

Six failure points, and the stage where each one starts.

Mismatched codingThe estimate is coded differently from the control budget, so you can't compare like with like.
Actuals onlyOnly actual costs are tracked, not commitments, so the overrun shows up months late.
After-the-fact POsPOs raised after the work has started bypass the budget check.
Unapproved VOsThe work is done but can't be billed.
Subjective progressLoosely measured progress inflates EV and hides CPI problems.
Terms mismatchBilling and payment terms don't line up, so the project runs cash-negative.
