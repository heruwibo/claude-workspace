- **10 redrawn charts and 21 tables.** These include the revenue series, interest as a share of resource revenue, the coal valuation build-up, the fund's structure diagram, and the roadmap.

**Evidence I added:**
- NRGI's 2020 study of 52 resource-backed loans worth US$164 miliar: 10 of the 14 borrowing countries hit debt trouble after the 2014 price crash.
- IMF and World Bank work on borrowing against future export earnings (the Pemex case).
- How Norway, Chile, Timor-Leste, Alaska and Kuwait run their resource funds.
- The Santiago Principles for sovereign wealth funds.
- The Pertamina crisis of 1975, and Angola's and Ghana's oil-backed loans.
- Constitutional Court ruling 36/PUU-X/2012, UU 1/2025 (Danantara), and the 2025 royalty regulations (PP 18 and 19/2025).
- Badan Geologi's coal reserves (31,95 miliar ton), USGS nickel data, and van der Ploeg (2011) on the resource curse.

**Problems in the source data, stated openly in the report:**
- **Rp10.000 T coal value:** the source treats Rp3.400 T as about 30% of the full value. That arithmetic gives about Rp11.356 T, and the 30% is never explained. The report treats Rp10.000 T as an upper bound only.
- **Revenue figures for 2004–2023:** these were read off the source chart, so they're approximate. The 2024 figures (Rp228,9 T and Rp488,4 T) are exact.
- **Cumulative interest:** I calculated it from the source's two series. It passes total resource revenue in 2022 and reaches about 114% by 2024.
- **Mineral revenue total:** the slide's figure leaves out one company (DAAZ).

**Labelled as the report's own proposal, not the source's:** the risk matrix, the month-by-month roadmap, the performance indicators and the 90-day actions.
