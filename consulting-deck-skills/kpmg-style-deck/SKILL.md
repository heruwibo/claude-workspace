# KPMG-style slide deck

Style inspired by publicly visible conventions of Big Four advisory decks. Do not use any firm's logo or trademarks; the look is emulated, not branded.

## Storyline
- Clear, evidence-based and risk-aware: situation → scope and approach → findings → implications → recommendations → roadmap.
- Emphasis on assurance, compliance, controls and measurable improvement; audience is often a board, audit committee or steering committee.
- Typical flow: Title → Agenda → Executive summary → Background and scope → Approach → Key findings → Recommendations → Implementation roadmap → Governance and next steps → Appendix.

## Slide anatomy
- **Title**: concise statement of the finding or message, 1–2 lines, left-aligned.
- Optional short lead-in sentence under the title framing the slide.
- Body: tables, process flows, maturity or control assessments, heat maps, phased roadmaps.
- Footer: source/notes, confidentiality or disclaimer line, slide number.

## Visual language
- White background with **deep blue** (~#00338D) as the anchor, medium blue (~#005EB8) and light blue/cyan (~#00A3A1 / #483698 sparingly) as supporting colors; greys for structure.
- Clean sans-serif; title ~24 pt, body 12–14 pt.
- Blue section-divider slides with large numerals; light, uncluttered content slides.
- Charts: simple columns/bars/lines in blue tints; highlight the key series in the darkest blue; RAG colors only for status.
- Consistent grid, ample margins, thin rules.

## Rules of thumb
- Link each finding to evidence, risk rating and recommendation.
- Define rating scales (e.g., High/Medium/Low, maturity 1–5) in a legend.
- Roadmaps: phases, owners, durations, dependencies, quick wins flagged.
- State assumptions and limitations; keep tone measured and precise.
- Max ~5 points per slide; move detail to the appendix.

## Workflow
1. Confirm scope, audience and whether the deck is findings-based, advisory or proposal.
2. Outline sections and draft the executive summary.
3. Build slides (pptx skill for .pptx); check legends, sources, consistent ratings and that every recommendation maps to a finding.
